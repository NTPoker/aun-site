The JLPT and speaking

Does the JLPT have a speaking test?
Three ways to check your spoken Japanese

AUN, run by 1900 Co., Ltd.　｜　Last updated：12 November 2026　｜　日本語

No. The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT) has no speaking test at any level from N1 to N5. Its sections are language knowledge, reading and listening, and there is no writing test either. The official site says there are currently no plans to add one (checked on 12 November 2026). So even if you have passed N2 or N1, your speaking has to be checked some other way.

What the JLPT measures

The JLPT has sections on language knowledge (vocabulary and grammar), reading, and listening. There is no speaking or writing section at any level from N1 to N5.

N1, N2Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar) and Reading; Listening
N3, N4, N5Language Knowledge (Vocabulary); Language Knowledge (Grammar) and Reading; Listening
How you answerMultiple choice, marked on an answer sheet
WhenTwice a year, in July and December. Outside Japan, some cities hold only one of the two

Asked whether the JLPT includes a conversation or composition test, the official FAQ answers that neither is currently included. It also says there are currently no plans to add speaking or writing tests (JLPT official FAQ, checked on 12 November 2026).

Why passing N2 or N1 does not necessarily mean you can speak

On the JLPT you pick the right answer from the choices given. When you speak, you have to recall the words yourself, build the sentence, and say it in sounds the other person can make out. There is a gap between words you understand when you read them and words that come out when you talk.

The JLPT publishes a “Can-do Self-Evaluation List”, and it includes items about speaking. But the list comes from a survey of what people who passed think they can do. It is not a measurement of speaking.

In short, a JLPT pass is evidence of language knowledge, reading and listening. It says nothing either way about how well you speak.

1. Take a speaking test

Some tests measure speaking only. One example is the ACTFL OPI: a live 15- to 30-minute phone conversation with a certified tester. It is offered in Japanese, and individuals can order it themselves through LTI.

If you need to submit a result to a school or an employer, ask them first which test they accept. Tests differ in where you can take them and in whether individuals can apply.

2. Ask a teacher for a level check

Japanese-language teachers are used to hearing learners. They can find your weak points and tell you what to study next. If you already take lessons, asking your teacher is the quickest way.

3. Have a Japanese person listen to a recording

Here a Japanese person who is not a teacher listens to your recording and tells you how it came across. In everyday conversation, people rarely point out the parts they did not catch. AUN (阿吽) is a test that gives you that feedback as a result.

What you can do with AUN

AUN is a Japanese speaking test that takes about 10 minutes on your phone. You answer questions out loud and get an AI review as soon as you finish (it has no scores). If you ask after the test, a Japanese person listens to your recording and sends back a result. You can ask once a week, and results usually arrive in a few days.

The Japanese listener’s result gives you scores for six skills (content, organisation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation), ○△× marks for 13 situations, and the one thing to fix next. You do not need to book, and it is free for now (first come, first served).

There are things it cannot do. There is one listener at present (Namba, who runs AUN), and it does not measure keigo or back-and-forth such as asking the other person to repeat. An AUN result is a guide, not official proof. You cannot submit it for a visa, admission or a job application. AUN scores do not correspond to JLPT levels N1 to N5 either. It does not replace the JLPT; you use it alongside the JLPT to find out how well you speak. More detail is in What is AUN?

Frequently asked questions

Are there plans to add a speaking test to the JLPT?

The official site says there are currently no plans to add one (checked on 12 November 2026). JLPT score reports now carry a CEFR level for reference, but the site says there are no plans to add speaking or writing tests to go with it.

Does passing N1 prove that I can speak Japanese?

No. N1 is evidence of language knowledge, reading and listening. The JLPT does not measure speaking.

Does the JFT-Basic have a speaking test?

No. The JFT-Basic has four sections: Script and Vocabulary, Conversation and Expression, Listening Comprehension, and Reading Comprehension. “Conversation and Expression” asks you to choose grammar and expressions used in conversation. You do not speak aloud.

Do AUN scores correspond to JLPT levels?

No. They measure different things: the JLPT looks at language knowledge, reading and listening, and AUN looks at speaking.
